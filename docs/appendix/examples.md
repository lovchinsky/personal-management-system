# Appendix: Starting the system and full examples

In this appendix: the first-setup scenario (Phases 1–5) and five worked configuration examples. The norm lives elsewhere: in [`docs/1-methodology.md`](../1-methodology.md) (the domain), [`docs/2-system.md`](../2-system.md) (mechanisms), [`docs/3-policy.md`](../3-policy.md) (policy) and [`contracts/canonical.yaml`](../../contracts/canonical.yaml) (lists of values). An example illustrates a rule but creates no requirements. Read it when setting up the system, and afterwards as needed.

## Starting the system

The first setup does not proceed top-down. A three-day workshop on Identity does not help a person with dozens of open commitments if they do not know what they have to do tomorrow. So adoption moves in a spiral through five phases.

### Phase 1 — Gain Control

- Capture current open loops.
- Clarify the most active commitments.
- Create Projects and Tasks where they are needed and give the Tasks real workflow statuses.
- A new committed Project that still needs clarification or design starts in status `Planning` instead of being disguised as a backlog idea.
- Set up a minimal Inbox, Calendar, Task Views and Review.

The goal is to stop depending on memory.

### Phase 2 — Map Reality

- Define the Areas of Focus.
- Mark the current `Mode` of each Area of Focus: `Advance | Maintain | Minimum`.
- Do not work out the idea of an acceptable state (`Standard`) in advance: it emerges in the first reviews.

The goal is to see the portfolio of responsibilities and the hidden neglect.

### Phase 3 — Establish Direction

- Formulate a few Identities and Principles.
- Identify the meaningful changes that need Objectives.
- Create Key Results as evidence, not as task lists.

The goal is to understand why some of the work takes priority.

### Phase 4 — Build Change Mechanisms

Review the Objectives and Areas of Focus by asking three questions:

- What needs a bounded Project?
- What needs a recurring Behavior System?
- What is already done well enough without a new mechanism?

Habits “for self-development” that have no function are not created.

### Phase 5 — Stabilize Feedback

- Weekly Control Review.
- Strategic Review.
- Behavioral and CBT diagnostics where friction recurs.

The goal is to stabilize the system, not to keep it a permanent implementation project.

### Top-down and bottom-up simultaneously

Top-down movement yields intention:

```text
Identity
→ Area of Focus
→ Objective
→ Change Mechanism
→ Task
```

Bottom-up movement yields learning:

```text
Task result
→ mechanism effectiveness
→ Key Result evidence
→ Objective validity
→ resource allocation across Areas of Focus
→ Identity assumption
```

An illustration of the two movements meeting. A person considers the Identity `Entrepreneur` important and has been creating venture Objectives for years, but the real pattern is different: product discovery, sales and uncertainty keep provoking strong aversion, while deep technical work produces steady interest and good results. The system does not explain this as “insufficient discipline”: it is evidence that may call for checking the Project design, the role, the Objective or even the initial Identity assumption. Higher Levels have greater inertia but are not immune to data: [“Higher Levels change slower”](../1-methodology.md#higher-levels-change-slower) and [“Feedback moves upward”](../1-methodology.md#feedback-moves-upward).

## Professional Development

**Situation.** A technical professional is developing competence in distributed systems, and competence as a desired change has to be told apart from learning materials as a tool.

**Configuration.** An Identity with Principles sets the orientation; the Area of Focus `Personal Development` is in mode `Advance`, and the idea of its acceptable state has taken shape over several reviews; the Objective describes the change, and its three Key Results are evidence of that change, not a list of what has been completed; a Project with a Definition of Done and a Behavior System are the mechanisms; the concrete work sits in a Task with status `Next Action`.

```text
Identity:
Learner / Strong Technical Professional

Principles:
- Seek difficult feedback.
- Prefer deliberate practice over passive consumption.
- Update beliefs when evidence changes.

Area of Focus:
Personal Development

Mode:
Advance

Standard:
Core professional competence remains current;
learning has a clear relation to real work.

Objective:
Develop advanced competence in distributed systems.

Key Results:
- Independently design and defend architecture for 3 relevant systems.
- Reduce a defined class of production performance incidents through improved design.
- Demonstrate competence through agreed review criteria, not course completion alone.

Project:
Build a production-grade distributed prototype.

Project Status:
Active

Definition of Done:
Prototype handles predefined load/failure scenarios and design decisions are documented.

Behavior System:
4 deliberate-practice sessions per regular week.

Task:
Reproduce failure scenario X locally and record observed bottleneck.
Status: Next Action
```

**What it illustrates.** “Finish the course” can be a Project or Project Support here, but not a Key Result: a course is an intervention/output, while competence is the desired change ([“7.1. A Key Result is not a Project”](../1-methodology.md#71-a-key-result-is-not-a-project)). The concrete work is represented by Tasks, because Next Action is a Task status, not a separate entity ([“11.2. Next Action as a status”](../1-methodology.md#112-next-action-as-a-status)).

## Personal Venture

**Situation.** A venture with an open commercial hypothesis: several parallel interventions, and some of the work depends on people outside the venture.

**Configuration.** One Objective about commercial viability; the Key Results are market metrics, not pieces of work; three Projects with statuses `Active` and `Planning`; two Behavior Systems provide recurring observation; the Tasks are in three different workflow statuses.

```text
Identity:
Builder

Area of Focus:
Personal Ventures

Mode:
Advance

Objective:
Determine and establish commercial viability of product X.

Key Results:
- MRR reaches target X.
- 90-day retention reaches target Y.
- Defined share of acquisition occurs without founder-led manual outreach.

Projects:
- Run problem-discovery cycle — Active
- Launch onboarding redesign — Active
- Implement referral mechanism — Planning

Behavior Systems:
- Review acquisition/retention data every Friday.
- Speak with at least one relevant user each week while discovery uncertainty remains.

Tasks:
- Draft interview guide for segment A       → Next Action
- Interview customer B on Thursday          → Scheduled
- Receive analytics access from contractor  → Waiting For
```

**What it illustrates.** A referral mechanism launched in line with its Definition of Done takes the Project to status `Done` even when the acquisition Key Result has not changed; in that case the strategic hypothesis is weakened ([“9.1. Definition of Done”](../1-methodology.md#91-definition-of-done), [“7.1. A Key Result is not a Project”](../1-methodology.md#71-a-key-result-is-not-a-project)). Here the Task statuses describe only the execution workflow, not the strategic outcome ([“11.1. Task Status”](../1-methodology.md#111-task-status)).

## Investments

**Situation.** A portfolio has to be kept within risk and liquidity boundaries, and the quality of decisions has to be separated from short-term returns.

**Configuration.** The Area of Focus `Investments` in mode `Advance` carries an ongoing responsibility, and its acceptable state has taken shape over the course of its reviews; the Objective and the possible Key Results address the decision-making process, not returns; the Project creates a tool, the Behavior System provides a recurring review, and the Task is a concrete step.

```text
Identity:
Investor

Principles:
- Think probabilistically.
- Separate thesis from price movement.
- Record reasoning before outcome is known.
- Protect downside before maximizing upside.

Area of Focus:
Investments

Mode:
Advance

Standard:
Portfolio remains within risk/liquidity boundaries;
theses are documented;
major positions are periodically reviewed.

Objective:
Improve decision quality and discipline of investment process.

Possible Key Results:
- All material allocation decisions have pre-decision thesis and risk case.
- A defined review sample shows fewer process violations.
- Portfolio risk remains within policy while expected-return criteria are maintained.

Project:
Create investment decision template and historical decision log.

Project Status:
Active

Behavior System:
Monthly portfolio/risk review.

Task:
Export last 12 months of transactions into decision-review dataset.
Status: Next Action
```

**What it illustrates.** A short-term return metric is not the only evidence of decision quality: an outcome can contain significant noise, so the Key Result architecture here matches the causality that the person can realistically assess ([“7.3. Leading and lagging evidence”](../1-methodology.md#73-leading-and-lagging-evidence)).

## Health without unnecessary OKR

**Situation.** A domain where keeping an acceptable state is enough: it needs no focused change, only reliable maintenance.

**Configuration.** The Area of Focus `Health` is in mode `Maintain`, and its acceptable state has taken shape over the course of its reviews; there is no Objective; maintenance comes from two Behavior Systems, steady training and an annual preventive review that remains a deliberate Routine; a one-off bounded screening is set up as a Project with Tasks, one of which waits for another and therefore has status `Waiting For` and, after booking, `Scheduled`.

```text
Identity:
Healthy Person

Area of Focus:
Health

Mode:
Maintain

Standard:
Adequate sleep and recovery;
regular physical activity;
preventive care not neglected;
relevant health indicators monitored appropriately.

Objective:
None currently.

Behavior Systems:
- Strength training three times per week   → Maintenance
- Annual preventive health review          → Maintenance, deliberate Routine

Project:
Complete this year's screening.

Project Status:
Active

Tasks:
- Book clinic appointment      → Next Action
- Attend screening             → Waiting For (dependency: appointment booked)

After booking:
- Attend screening             → Scheduled (appointment time)
```

**What it illustrates.** Not every Area of Focus needs an Objective: here maintenance lives in the `Standard`, the mode and the Behavior Systems ([“6.3. When an Area of Focus needs an Objective”](../1-methodology.md#63-when-an-area-of-focus-needs-an-objective), [“6.2. Mode: Advance, Maintain, Minimum”](../1-methodology.md#62-mode-advance-maintain-minimum)). The stability of the training does not remove its instances: each workout exists as a Task, and completions, failed instances and the streak are derived from them; only the effort of confirming is simplified ([“10.9. Behavior System and Tasks”](../1-methodology.md#109-behavior-system-and-tasks), [“10.7. Tracking”](../1-methodology.md#107-tracking)). The annual review in `Maintenance` remains a Routine: lifecycle and automaticity are different dimensions ([“10.1. Lifecycle and automaticity — different dimensions”](../1-methodology.md#101-lifecycle-and-automaticity--different-dimensions)).

## A system built wrong and its redesign

An antipattern: a mistake in a person's configuration and its correction.

**Built this way.**

```text
Objective: Be productive
Key Result: Complete 50 tasks/week
Projects:
- Health
- Career
- Learning

Daily calendar:
08:00 read
08:30 exercise
09:10 email
09:30 Project A task 1
10:00 task 2
...

Habit tracker:
water / reading / exercise / journal / meditation / planning / ...
```

**Why this does not work.** The problems here are not cosmetic. `Be productive` does not describe a meaningful change. `50 tasks` incentivizes throughput regardless of value. `Health` and `Career` are Areas of Focus, not Projects. The Calendar mixes the hard landscape with a wish list. The Habit tracker instruments a large part of everyday life with no obvious decision value.

**How to do it right.**

```text
Areas of Focus:
- Career — `Mode: Advance`
- Health — `Mode: Maintain`
- Personal Development — `Mode: Advance`

Objective:
Increase professional leverage through capability X.

Key Result:
Evidence of capability/result Y.

Projects:
Two bounded interventions supporting the Key Result.

Behavior Systems:
One deliberate-practice system.
One health-maintenance system.

Tasks:
One shared task model with status:
Next Action | Scheduled | Waiting For | Someday/Maybe | Done | Canceled | Failed

Calendar:
Hard commitments + protected deep-work blocks.

Execution:
A short contextual Available Now list from Next Action Tasks.
```

**What it illustrates.** Four of the problems listed above are named in [“26. Typical failure modes”](../1-methodology.md#26-typical-failure-modes): Key Results as task lists, Projects without endings, Overplanning and Habit tracker as second job. Fewer objects do not mean less control; often fewer objects make a better model of reality.
